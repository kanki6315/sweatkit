# Wave 1 — source accuracy

5 November 2026. Scope: Health ingestion, source preservation, and calculation verification. No presentation files or approved boards changed. No live phone or simulator checks claimed.

## Fixes

- Preserve Health lap events in `RecordedActivity.laps`. Modern explicit intervals retain gaps; old zero-duration markers end the lap starting at the preceding lap end or workout start. Duplicate events are deterministic, out-of-workout/overlapping lap intervals are rejected, and no trailing lap is invented.
- Preserve Health segment events independently in **additive optional `RecordedActivity.segments: [RecordedLap]?`**. Old recorded caches and Health snapshots decode with nil. Overlapping segments remain separate; they are not kilometre splits. Binary stream cache format 3 adds these intervals after the native sample columns; versions 1 and 2 remain readable. No database schema or LibraryModel changes.
- Shared distance interval boundaries now emit one native observation, preventing a shared subsecond boundary from receiving two votes in the normaliser's per-second average. Example: boundaries at 0 s/0 m and 0.25 s/1 m average to 0.5 m, not 0.667 m.
- Add optional `HealthSyncState.snapshotAdapterVersion`. Adapter version 1 durably queues known workouts once, preserving anchors and authorization. This refreshes existing recordings with intervals and corrected boundaries; failed pending snapshots remain retryable. The existing schema-3 permission review still requests distance/steps/running form and queues all known workouts.

## Calculation and source review

- Quantity-series queries expand individual Health entries, scoped to both workout association and its writing source. Workout totals are never spread over GPS. Overlap in cumulative quantity intervals rejects the channel rather than double counting. Long distance gaps remain missing; invalid intervals remain rejected. Source name and bundle ID remain in snapshots.
- Cadence is steps/minute for Health on-foot data: `steps * 60 / interval seconds`, emitted only inside intervals of at most five seconds. Longer step aggregates cannot establish cursor cadence. FIT on-foot cadence still doubles strides into steps. A Fitness average cadence does not prove cursor cadence exists.
- Heart rate interpolates only across at most five missing grid seconds, with readings on both sides and no pause boundary. Six missing seconds and trailing gaps stay unavailable even if an activity average/chart exists. No invented HR thresholds were used.
- Elapsed time is the whole-second grid; timer time removes explicit pauses; outdoor moving time additionally removes recorded speed below 0.5 m/s on foot (1 m/s cycling). Missing speed counts as moving. Incomplete pause positions keep stated timer time as summary-only and make moving time/splits unavailable. Existing tests cover these cases.
- Splits use timer seconds, while feed average pace uses moving seconds/distance. The current split convention assigns each grid second by cumulative distance, carries the last distance through gaps, includes a final partial split, and may produce a zero-distance final split at an exact kilometre boundary. These existing, tested conventions were not silently changed to mimic another app. Native samples exactly at the end are outside the grid; last stream distance can differ from the source summary. Health feed rows prefer stated workout distance where available.
- On-foot ascent uses the existing 3 m reversal threshold. Fitness's 5 m gain cannot be forced onto that calculation without the native altitude series and knowledge of Apple's filtering.
- A thinner snapshot after changed read access becomes a new revision; earlier originals remain stored. Restoring readable data refreshes the same UUID and does not duplicate it. Empty workout reads do not imply deletion or denied permission. Explicit Health deletion retains the local recording.
- Background delivery remains acknowledged after durable anchors/pending capture; cancellation stops queries and resumes waiters once. No change to that implementation was needed.
